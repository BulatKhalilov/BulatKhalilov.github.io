About

Bulat Khalilov is an ethnographer, researcher, journalist, curator and co-founder of Ored Recordings, a label working with traditional and local music of the North Caucasus and beyond.

I develop and produce projects at the intersection of traditional and independent music, ethnography and contemporary art. Much of my work is concerned with cultural phenomena that remain outside dominant cultural agendas: how they are represented, contextualized and connected to broader social and political processes.

Over the years, I have participated in activities at SOAS University of London, the University of Vienna, the Higher School of Economics and the Philharmonic Society of Rouen as a visiting researcher, invited speaker or collaborator.

Research

Despite coming from a non-academic background, I have been professionally engaged with decolonial approaches to music, culture and artistic practice for many years.

A central question in my work is how the traditional music of the peoples of the North Caucasus exists today: how it participates in the formation and preservation of identity, how it creates horizontal connections, and how it changes when entering new cultural, institutional and economic environments.

New trajectories of traditional music

At the University of Göttingen, I research how traditional music enters contemporary European and global music infrastructures.

Drawing on interviews, academic and journalistic literature, artistic practices and my own professional experience, I examine the role of labels, festivals, media, institutions, curators and musicians in this transformation.

A particular focus of the project is the relationship between these cultural shifts and broader colonial and capitalist hierarchies.

Ored Recordings

My main long-term project is Ored Recordings, which I co-founded with Timur Kodzoko.

Ored documents, studies, publishes and promotes traditional and local music of the North Caucasus through recordings, media projects, texts, events, research and collaborations with contemporary cultural institutions and music scenes.

The project explores traditional music not as a static heritage object, but as a living cultural practice connected to identity, migration, memory and contemporary cultural production.

Georgia & Germany

Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, I moved to Georgia together with my colleague Timur Kodzoko. There, I worked as a visiting researcher at Ilia State University and participated in organizing a North Caucasian community initiative.

Within this group, we organized lectures, meetings, workshops and two festivals presenting arts and grassroots initiatives from the North Caucasus.

In 2024, our families moved to Germany. I continued working with the European independent music scene and Circassian diaspora communities, organizing concerts and other cultural projects.

In 2025, Timur Kodzoko and I received support from the Right Livelihood Foundation. With this support, I joined the Department of Cultural Anthropology / European Ethnology at the University of Göttingen and the Centre for Global Migration Studies (CeMig).

Writing

As a journalist, I write about traditional music, decolonial discourse, cultural representation and processes of self-exoticization.

My journalistic work has become an important foundation for my academic research and allows me to work between academic, artistic and independent cultural contexts.
