Ored Recordings

Ored Recordings is an independent label, archive and cultural platform that I co-founded with Timur Kodzoko in Nalchik in 2014.

The project documents, studies, publishes and promotes traditional and local music of the North Caucasus through field recordings, music releases, archival projects, journalism, research, events and collaborations.

Ored approaches traditional music not as a static heritage object, but as a living cultural practice connected with identity, migration, memory, political transformation and contemporary artistic production.

Ored Ensemble

Ored Ensemble is a collaboration between Düsseldorf-based label TAL and the North Caucasian, Göttingen-based platform Ored Recordings.

Taking traditional Circassian music as its foundation — from songs of the Russo-Caucasian War to ritual and dance melodies — the ensemble explores electroacoustic improvisation, abstract soundscapes and richly detailed compositions.

While many Ored Recordings projects have a specific, often anthropological character and seek to document music close to its original social context, Ored Ensemble treats traditional material as free sonic practice.

It is an attempt to liberate Circassian music from the category of ethnic heritage and imagine new trajectories and possible futures.

The musicians deliberately rethink the familiar opposition between “folk” and “experimental.” Circassian shichepshin and healing melodies can serve as the basis for sonic experiments, while electronic instruments can themselves represent established traditions such as free jazz, sound art or the Düsseldorf school of experimental music.

The ensemble began as a duo between Timur Kodzoko, founder of the post-traditional Circassian group Jrpjej, and Stefan Schneider, known for groups including to rococo rot and Kreidler.

It later expanded into a trio with Berlin-based cellist, composer and experimental artist Martina Bertoni. Danish trombonist Maria Bertel and Italian vocalist and flutist Chiara Cecconcello also contributed to the recording of the first album.

Through sonic and artistic methods, Ored Ensemble creates a political proposition about new possibilities, geographies and structures.

Ulyap Songs: Beyond Circassian Tradition

Ulyap Songs: Beyond Circassian Tradition explores the phenomenon of late traditional Circassian music situated at the intersection of ritual melodies, drinking songs and late-Soviet criminal ballads.

The project is a collaboration between Ored Recordings, the FLEE Project publishing platform and independent curator Nikita Rasskazov.

Based on materials from field expeditions, archival research and numerous interviews, the project resulted in a book and a music album combining Circassian music with reinterpretations by experimental musicians from an international context.

The project documents the local song tradition characteristic of Ulyap village in the Republic of Adygea and the broader genre of Circassian women’s songs.

At the same time, it addresses cultural hybridity, resistance to colonialism and the processes through which ethnic myths are created and reproduced.

I participated in the project as one of its founders, as a curator and organizer of field expeditions, liner producer, researcher and writer.

The project was realized with the support of the Pro Helvetia Foundation, Switzerland.

Music Post-traditional

Jrpjej

Jrpjej is a Circassian music project working with traditional musical practices and contemporary approaches to sound.

Rather than treating traditional material as a source for stylistic fusion, the project explores how older musical practices can exist within new artistic and social contexts.

Jrpjej is closely connected to a broader idea of post-traditional music: practices in which tradition remains active and meaningful while being recontextualized through contemporary performance, recording and listening environments.

New Trajectories of Traditional Music

My current research examines how traditional music enters contemporary European and global music infrastructures.

I focus on the role of independent labels, curators, festivals, venues, journalists, academics and musicians in creating new contexts for traditional and post-traditional music.

The project also examines the relationship between these cultural transformations and broader colonial and capitalist hierarchies.

The research is conducted at the Institute of Cultural Anthropology / European Ethnology (KAEE), University of Göttingen, with support from the Right Livelihood Foundation.

Georgia Community · Culture

North Caucasus Community in Georgia

After moving to Georgia following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, I participated in organizing a North Caucasian community initiative together with colleagues and friends.

The initiative created a space for meetings, lectures, discussions and workshops and sought to connect people from different parts of the North Caucasus living in Georgia.

As part of this work, we organized two festivals presenting contemporary art, music and grassroots initiatives connected with the North Caucasus.

University of Göttingen Teaching

Decoloniality, Music, and Power

Together with Timur Kodzoko, I developed and taught a course at the University of Göttingen exploring the relationship between music, coloniality, representation and power.

The course brought decolonial theory into dialogue with contemporary musical practices and questions surrounding traditional music, cultural institutions, identity and representation.
