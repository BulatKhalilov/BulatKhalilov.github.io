Ored Recordings

Ored Recordings is an independent label, archive and cultural platform that I co-founded with Timur Kodzoko in Nalchik in 2014.

The project documents, studies, publishes and promotes traditional and local music of the North Caucasus. Its work includes field recordings, music releases, archival projects, texts, events, research and collaborations with musicians and cultural institutions.

Ored approaches traditional music not as a fixed or isolated heritage object, but as a living cultural practice that continues to change through migration, contemporary artistic production, political transformation and new forms of cultural circulation.

Jrpjej

Jrpjej is a music project working with Circassian traditional music and contemporary approaches to sound.

Rather than treating traditional material as a source for stylistic fusion, the project explores how older musical practices can exist in new artistic and social contexts.

Jrpjej is part of a broader interest in what I describe as post-traditional music: practices in which tradition remains active and meaningful while being recontextualized through contemporary performance, recording and listening environments.

Research

New Trajectories of Traditional Music

My current research project examines how traditional music enters contemporary European and global music infrastructures.

I focus particularly on the role of independent labels, curators, festivals, venues, journalists, academics and musicians in shaping new contexts for traditional and post-traditional music.

The project draws on interviews, academic and journalistic literature, artistic practices and my own experience of working within independent music scenes.

The research is conducted at the Institute of Cultural Anthropology / European Ethnology (KAEE), University of Göttingen, with support from the Right Livelihood Foundation.

North Caucasus Community in Georgia

After moving to Georgia following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, I participated in organizing a North Caucasian community initiative together with colleagues and friends.

The initiative created a space for meetings, lectures, discussions and workshops and sought to connect people from different parts of the North Caucasus living in Georgia.

As part of this work, we organized two festivals presenting contemporary art, music and grassroots initiatives connected with the North Caucasus.

Teaching

Decoloniality, Music, and Power

Together with Timur Kodzoko, I developed and taught a course at the University of Göttingen exploring the relationship between music, coloniality, representation and power.

The course brought decolonial theory into dialogue with contemporary musical practices and with questions surrounding traditional music, cultural institutions, identity and representation.
